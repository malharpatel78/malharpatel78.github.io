03b The SQL work

Five projects. Real questions, real queries.

Each one starts with a messy public dataset, logs every cleaning fix, answers business questions in SQL and ends with recommendations and an interactive dashboard where every chart shows the query behind it.

Direct marketing · 2,240 customers · 6 campaigns

Marketing Campaign Response

Every mass send lost money: a campaign needs 27% response to break even and the best reached 15%. Targeting only the top 10% to 20% of customers by a simple score would have turned a $2,731 loss into about $760 profit.

UK online retailer · 541,909 invoice lines

Customer RFM Segmentation

Champions are 15% of customers but 51% of revenue, and 9.6% of revenue sits with customers drifting away. Includes a ranked win-back list and cohort retention. Cleaning caught a £168K order that was cancelled minutes later.

SQLiteRFM with NTILECohort retentionCTEsWindow functions
Live dashboardSQL on GitHub
Retail analytics · 8,523 product listings · 10 outlets

Blinkit Grocery Sales

Assortment size, not city tier, store size or age, explains the gap between stores: every outlet sells about the same per product stocked. Products stocked in 9 outlets sell about 11 times more than those in one.

Marketplace analytics · 8,652 restaurants

Zomato Restaurant Deep Dive

Fixing 2,139 placeholder ratings moved the India average from 2.77 to 3.35. Destination districts rate best, common cuisines rate below average, and a vote-weighted rating surfaces the real best-value spots.

Swiggy Restaurant Market

Splitting a messy city field collapsed 821 apparent cities into 551 real ones and changed the ranking. Seven cities hold half of all listings, and the most common cuisines are not the best rated.
